Frequently Asked Questions (2026 – Nepal Context)

How much does a home solar system cost in Nepal right now?

4–6 kW typical household system: NPR 2.8–4.8 lakh (grid-tied without battery). Off-grid with battery adds NPR 1–2 lakh. Prices vary by brand (Chinese/Tier-1), installer, and location. Government subsidy may reduce cost by NPR 20,000–40,000 for eligible systems (check AEPC rules).

Is net metering still available in Nepal?

Yes — reinstated and improved in recent years. You can export excess power to NEA grid (usually at ~NPR 5–6 per unit). Works best for systems up to 500 kW. Apply through your local NEA office after installation.

How much electricity will my system produce in Nepal?

1 kW system → 4.5–5.5 units/day on average (1,600–2,000 units/year). Higher in sunny Terai, slightly lower in hilly areas. Cloudy/monsoon days reduce output to 20–60%, but annual total remains good.

What's the payback period for solar in Nepal?

Usually 4–7 years depending on system size, electricity usage, and net metering. After payback → 15–20+ years of very low-cost or free electricity (panels last 25–30 years).

Do solar panels work during load-shedding or at night?

Grid-tied systems stop during power cuts (safety feature). Hybrid (with battery) or off-grid systems continue. At night → use grid or stored battery power.

Are there government subsidies or loans for solar?

Yes — AEPC offers subsidies (especially rural/off-grid), VAT exemptions on quality-certified panels/inverters, and low-interest loans via banks (sometimes 50% interest subsidy for few years). Urban rooftop systems mostly benefit from net metering rather than direct subsidy.

How long do solar panels and inverters last?

Panels: 25–30 year performance warranty (still ~80–85% efficient after 25 years). Inverters: 8–15 years (string inverters cheaper, microinverters longer life).

Is maintenance difficult or expensive?

No — mostly cleaning 3–6 times/year + occasional inspection. Annual cost usually NPR 5,000–15,000 for average home system. Much cheaper than frequent grid/generator issues.
